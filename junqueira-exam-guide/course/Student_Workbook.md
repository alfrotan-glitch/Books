# ANSWER KEY AND FEEDBACK — STUDENT WORKBOOK

The answers below reflect the author's original recall paraphrases and are intended to be cross-checked in the corresponding chapter of *Junqueira's Basic Histology*, 17th ed. Do not treat numerical estimates or professional examination scope as guaranteed unchanged.

## Chapter 1 — Histology & Its Methods of Study

1. **What does formalin do to a tissue?** Cross-links proteins, preserving structure while making the tissue firm enough to section.
2. **Which dye stains nuclei, and why?** Hematoxylin, a basic dye that binds acidic nucleic acids (DNA and RNA).
3. **What does PAS demonstrate?** Carbohydrates, glycogen, and glycoprotein-rich structures such as basement membranes.
4. **Light microscope versus electron microscope resolution?** About 0.2 µm versus about 3 nm in routine sections, roughly two orders of magnitude.
5. **Why is a 5-µm section dangerous to interpret in one glance?** It samples a single plane; a tube can look like a ring, a sphere like a circle, and a fold like a septum.
6. **Comparison model:** LM preserves architecture at lower resolution; TEM resolves subcellular ultrastructure but requires ultrathin tissue.
7. **Visual evidence:** Reconstruct the chain fixation → section → stain and identify the point where a false tissue fold may arise. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A stain's color alone cannot prove the identity of a specific protein.

## Chapter 2 — The Cytoplasm

1. **Rough versus smooth ER — what distinguishes them functionally?** Rough ER carries ribosomes and makes membrane and secretory proteins; smooth ER makes lipids and steroids and detoxifies.
2. **Why is the Golgi described as polarized?** Cis face receives, medial cisternae modify, trans face packages and sorts.
3. **Which organelle contains catalase, and what does it do?** The peroxisome; catalase breaks down hydrogen peroxide, and peroxisomes also perform β-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids.
4. **Which cytoskeletal element forms the mitotic spindle, and which motor walks toward its plus end?** Microtubules; kinesin moves toward the plus end (anterograde), dynein toward the minus end (retrograde).
5. **What ultrastructural feature identifies a cell that secretes steroid hormone?** Abundant smooth ER and lipid droplets, with mitochondria that have tubular cristae.
6. **Comparison model:** Plasma cells rely on rough ER/Golgi; steroid-secreting cells rely on smooth ER and lipid droplets.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw the antibody secretory route; place a lysosome and mitochondrion. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A basal body contains nine microtubule triplets; a primary cilium usually has a 9+0 doublet-based axoneme.

## Chapter 3 — The Nucleus

1. **What is a nucleosome?** About 150 bp of DNA wrapped around an octamer of histones (two each of H2A, H2B, H3, H4), with H1 on the outside.
2. **Heterochromatin versus euchromatin?** Heterochromatin is condensed and transcriptionally silent; euchromatin is dispersed and active.
3. **What is a Barr body?** The facultatively heterochromatic, inactivated X chromosome in a female somatic cell.
4. **Which checkpoint does p53 guard, and what happens if repair fails?** The G1 checkpoint; p53 triggers apoptosis if the DNA cannot be repaired.
5. **Name two ways apoptosis differs from necrosis.** Apoptosis is ATP-dependent, shrinks the cell, keeps the membrane intact, and causes no inflammation; necrosis is classically passive — it swells and ruptures the cell and inflames the tissue — although regulated lytic deaths such as necroptosis blur the boundary.
6. **Comparison model:** Apoptosis usually involves cell shrinkage and controlled fragmentation; necrosis has membrane disruption and typically inflammation.
7. **Visual evidence:** Arrange G1, S, G2 and M on an annotated cycle and mark DNA synthesis. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A condensed mitotic chromosome does not imply DNA is being replicated.

## Chapter 4 — Epithelial Tissue

1. **How do you classify an epithelium in one pass?** First count the layers (simple, stratified, pseudostratified), then name the surface cell shape (squamous, cuboidal, columnar).
2. **What does a tight junction do besides sealing?** It fences membrane proteins, creating and maintaining apical–basal polarity.
3. **Which junction attaches a cell to the basal lamina?** The hemidesmosome.
4. **Microvillus, stereocilium, cilium — core structure?** Microvillus and stereocilium: actin-based; most motile epithelial cilia: 9 + 2 axoneme; typical primary cilium: 9 + 0 axoneme; a basal body has nine microtubule triplets.
5. **Why is urothelium suited to the bladder?** Umbrella cells with uroplakin plaques and tight junctions form an osmotic barrier, and the epithelium thins as the bladder fills.
6. **Comparison model:** Pseudostratified epithelia appear layered because nuclei lie at different heights, but all cells reach basal lamina; stratified epithelia contain multiple actual layers.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch a free surface, basal lamina and lateral cells for each type; identify one source of tangential-section artifact. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A swollen urothelial umbrella cell may flatten when the bladder is distended.

## Chapter 5 — Connective Tissue

1. **Which collagen type forms the basal lamina?** Type IV (with laminin), over a reticular lamina of type III.
2. **Which collagen type is defective in osteogenesis imperfecta?** Type I.
3. **What is the only unsulfated GAG?** Hyaluronan.
4. **How do you tell dense regular from dense irregular connective tissue?** Direction: regular has parallel bundles (tendon), irregular has randomly oriented bundles (dermis).
5. **What does a plasma cell look like, and what does it make?** Eccentric clock-face nucleus with a pale perinuclear Golgi halo; it secretes antibody.
6. **Comparison model:** Dense regular connective tissue has closely aligned collagen bundles for unidirectional tensile load; loose connective tissue has more open ground-substance spaces.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch parallel tendon collagen next to multidirectional areolar matrix; mark fibroblast nuclei. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Not all tissue-resident macrophages arise from circulating adult monocytes.

## Chapter 6 — Adipose Tissue

1. **One histological difference between white and brown fat?** White: one large droplet, peripheral flattened nucleus. Brown: many small droplets, central round nucleus, eosinophilic granular cytoplasm.
2. **What is UCP1 and where does it act?** Uncoupling protein 1 in the inner mitochondrial membrane; it lets protons leak back without ATP synthesis, releasing heat.
3. **Name two adipokines.** Leptin (signals stored energy) and adiponectin (increases insulin sensitivity).
4. **When is brown fat most abundant?** In the newborn (neck, back, mediastinum); it persists in adult neck and supraclavicular depots.
5. **What is lipotoxicity?** When adipose storage capacity is exceeded, lipid accumulates in liver, muscle, and pancreas, impairing their function.
6. **Comparison model:** White adipocytes are usually unilocular energy stores with peripheral nuclei; brown adipocytes are multilocular and mitochondria-rich, specialized for non-shivering thermogenesis.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw a single large fat droplet next to many small lipid droplets; place nuclei. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Lipid appears optically empty after routine paraffin processing, not because adipocytes contain only water.

## Chapter 7 — Cartilage

1. **Which cartilage has no perichondrium, and why does that matter?** Fibrocartilage; without a perichondrial source of new chondroblasts, disc and meniscal injuries heal poorly.
2. **Which collagen types are in hyaline and fibrocartilage?** Hyaline: type II. Fibrocartilage: type I and type II.
3. **What is an isogenous group?** Daughter chondrocytes from a recent division, still sharing one lacuna.
4. **Appositional versus interstitial growth?** Appositional adds cartilage at the periphery and increases width; interstitial growth divides chondrocytes inside lacunae, increases length, and occurs only in young cartilage.
5. **Why is articular cartilage a poor repairer?** It is avascular, aneural, and lacks perichondrium, so nutrition depends on synovial fluid diffusion.
6. **Comparison model:** Hyaline cartilage usually has homogeneous type II-collagen matrix; fibrocartilage has coarse collagen bundles and often rows of chondrocytes.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw an isogenous group and aligned chondrocyte rows; show the perichondrium only where anatomically expected. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Articular cartilage and fibrocartilage generally lack perichondrium.

## Chapter 8 — Bone

1. **Which cell makes bone matrix, and which resorbs it?** Osteoblasts make osteoid; osteoclasts resorb bone using a ruffled border and acid.
2. **What does osteoid contain, and what mineralizes it?** Type I collagen and proteoglycans; hydroxyapatite crystals deposit along the collagen.
3. **How do osteocytes stay alive inside lacunae?** Their processes run through canaliculi and contact processes of neighboring cells and the central canal, allowing diffusion of nutrients.
4. **Endochondral versus intramembranous ossification?** Endochondral replaces a cartilage model (long bones, base of skull); intramembranous forms directly in mesenchyme (flat skull bones, mandible).
5. **What happens at a growth plate, zone by zone?** Reserve → proliferative → hypertrophic → calcified matrix → ossification, with chondrocyte death and vascular invasion.
6. **Comparison model:** Osteoblasts deposit osteoid on forming surfaces; multinucleated osteoclasts resorb bone in Howship lacunae.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch osteon central canal, lamellae, lacunae and canaliculi. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Osteoclasts are not osteocytes multiplied in place; they derive from monocyte/macrophage-lineage progenitors.

## Chapter 9 — Nerve Tissue & the Nervous System

1. **Which glial cell myelinates the CNS, and which myelinates the PNS?** Oligodendrocyte (CNS, several axons); Schwann cell (PNS, one segment of one axon).
2. **Which CNS glia act as resident macrophages, and where do they originate?** Microglia; mainly from embryonic yolk-sac myeloid precursors rather than the adult circulating monocyte pool.
3. **What forms the blood–brain barrier?** Tight junctions between capillary endothelial cells, reinforced by astrocyte end-feet and pericytes.
4. **How is a signal carried along an axon?** An action potential; myelin allows saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier.
5. **Why does CNS injury scar rather than regenerate?** Oligodendrocytes and myelin inhibit axon growth, astrocytes form a glial scar, and there is no permissive sheath like the PNS bands of Büngner.
6. **Comparison model:** One oligodendrocyte can form segments on multiple CNS axons; a myelinating Schwann cell makes one PNS internode of one axon.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw an axon, myelin internode, node of Ranvier and endoneurium; distinguish glial wrapping from connective tissue. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Most homeostatic microglia arise from embryonic yolk-sac myeloid precursors.

## Chapter 10 — Muscle Tissue

1. **Which band does not change length during contraction?** The A band, because it is the length of the thick filaments.
2. **What hides the myosin-binding site at rest, and what moves it?** Tropomyosin; calcium binds troponin C and shifts tropomyosin.
3. **Cardiac versus skeletal muscle at the T-tubule level?** Cardiac diads lie at the Z line; skeletal triads lie at the A–I junction.
4. **What is the calcium sensor in smooth muscle?** Calmodulin, which activates myosin light-chain kinase; smooth muscle has no troponin.
5. **Which muscle types regenerate?** Skeletal (limited, from satellite cells), smooth (hyperplasia/hypertrophy); cardiac does not.
6. **Comparison model:** Skeletal fibers are striated and multinucleated with peripheral nuclei; smooth-muscle cells are nonstriated with central nuclei and use calmodulin/MLCK.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch the A band, I band, H zone and Z lines in a sarcomere. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** The A band remains constant in sliding-filament contraction.

## Chapter 11 — The Circulatory System

1. **Three layers of a vessel wall, outside in?** Tunica adventitia, tunica media, tunica intima.
2. **Which vessel has a media of only one or two smooth-muscle layers and controls peripheral resistance?** The arteriole.
3. **Which capillary type is fenestrated, and where is it found?** Fenestrated capillaries are in endocrine glands, gut mucosa, and kidney; they allow rapid exchange.
4. **What distinguishes a vein from an artery in section?** Veins have a larger, often collapsed lumen and a thinner wall with less muscle; valves are present in medium veins.
5. **Where does lymph begin and end?** Blind-ended lymphatic capillaries in tissue; it drains through nodes to the thoracic duct and into the subclavian vein.
6. **Comparison model:** Arteries typically have proportionally thicker tunica media; veins often have thin media and a relatively prominent adventitia, but section shape alone is unreliable.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch intima, media and adventitia in each vessel; add an internal elastic lamina where expected. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A collapsed vein can be round or narrow; do not identify a vessel only from lumen shape.

## Chapter 12 — Blood

1. **How does serum differ from plasma?** Serum is plasma after clotting, so it lacks fibrinogen and consumed clotting factors.
2. **Normal erythrocyte dimensions and lifespan?** About 7.5 µm diameter, biconcave with a 2.6-µm rim, circulating about 120 days.
3. **Which leukocyte is most numerous, and which is largest?** Neutrophils (~50–70%); monocytes are the largest.
4. **What do eosinophil granules contain?** Major basic protein and eosinophil cationic protein, active against parasites and in allergy.
5. **What are the four steps of hemostasis?** Vascular spasm, primary hemostasis (platelet plug), coagulation (fibrin clot), and clot retraction with repair.
6. **Comparison model:** Eosinophils usually show bilobed nuclei and eosinophilic granules; neutrophils commonly show multilobed nuclei and finer neutral-staining granules.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch an eosinophil, neutrophil, basophil and monocyte; label two identifying features each. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A human mature erythrocyte is anucleate; do not apply the generic nucleated-cell diagram.

## Chapter 13 — Hemopoiesis

1. **Where does hemopoiesis occur in the adult?** Red marrow of the axial skeleton (vertebrae, ribs, sternum, pelvis, proximal femur).
2. **What is the first recognizable erythroid precursor, and when is the nucleus lost?** The proerythroblast; the nucleus is extruded at the orthochromatophilic erythroblast stage, leaving a reticulocyte that still contains residual RNA.
3. **How do you recognize a megakaryocyte?** A huge polyploid cell with a lobulated nucleus whose cytoplasm fragments into platelets.
4. **Which growth factor drives erythroid production?** Erythropoietin, made by peritubular interstitial cells of the kidney.
5. **What does the M:E ratio describe?** The ratio of myeloid (granulocytic) to erythroid precursors in marrow, normally about 2:1 to 4:1.
6. **Comparison model:** Multipotent hematopoietic stem cells self-renew and give rise to multiple blood lineages; megakaryocytes produce platelet fragments.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw the erythrocyte maturation series and mark nucleus extrusion. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Hematopoietic stem cells are multipotent, not normally pluripotent.

## Chapter 14 — The Immune System & Lymphoid Organs

1. **Which MHC class is on all nucleated cells, and which T cell reads it?** MHC I; CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells.
2. **Which cells typically present peptides on MHC II?** Professional antigen-presenting cells: dendritic cells, macrophages, and B cells; thymic epithelial cells also present peptides during T-cell selection.
3. **Where do T cells undergo positive and negative selection?** Positive selection in the cortex; negative selection in the medulla.
4. **What is the difference between the thymus and the spleen in one line?** The thymus makes and selects T cells and has no follicles; the spleen filters blood and has no afferent lymphatics.
5. **What does an FDC do?** Follicular dendritic cells display antigen–antibody complexes to B cells and drive germinal-center formation.
6. **Comparison model:** Thymus has lobules, cortex/medulla and Hassall corpuscles; lymph nodes have follicles, subcapsular sinuses and medullary cords.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch a lymph node and thymic lobule without labels; ask a partner to identify them. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Hassall corpuscles are not germinal centers; the thymus is not organized into ordinary lymphoid follicles.

## Chapter 15 — Digestive Tract

1. **Name the four layers of the gut wall in order.** Mucosa, submucosa, muscularis, serosa or adventitia.
2. **Which cells secrete HCl and intrinsic factor?** Parietal cells; they have intracellular canaliculi and many mitochondria.
3. **What are the three surface amplifiers of the small intestine?** Plicae circulares, villi, and microvilli.
4. **How do lipids leave the villus?** As chylomicrons through the lacteal, not through capillaries.
5. **Which segment has teniae coli and no villi?** The colon (large intestine).
6. **Comparison model:** Duodenum is supported by Brunner glands in submucosa; ileum has villi and may show Peyer patches.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw the four layers of gut wall and place Meissner and Auerbach plexuses. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** A superficial mucosal biopsy lacking submucosa cannot reliably exclude duodenum by missing Brunner glands.

## Chapter 16 — Organs Associated with the Digestive Tract

1. **Which salivary gland is purely serous?** The parotid. Submandibular is mixed, mainly serous; sublingual is mixed, mainly mucous.
2. **What do striated ducts do?** Reabsorb Na⁺ and Cl⁻ and add HCO₃⁻, making saliva hypotonic.
3. **What do centroacinar cells secrete, and under what hormone?** Bicarbonate-rich fluid, under secretin.
4. **Trace bile from canaliculus to duodenum.** Canaliculi → canals of Hering → bile ductules → interlobular ducts → hepatic ducts → common hepatic duct → cystic duct → gallbladder → cystic duct → common bile duct → hepatopancreatic ampulla.
5. **Which way does blood flow in a hepatic lobule?** From portal areas at the periphery toward the central vein.
6. **Comparison model:** Liver has hepatic plates and sinusoidal channels; pancreas has exocrine acini and pale endocrine islets.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch one liver lobule including portal triad and central vein; draw arrows for blood and bile in opposite directions. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Do not assume pancreatic endocrine islets are mucous acini.

## Chapter 17 — The Respiratory System

1. **What marks the transition from conducting to respiratory airway?** The appearance of alveoli in the wall — the respiratory bronchiole.
2. **Which cells line almost the entire alveolar surface?** Type I pneumocytes.
3. **What do type II pneumocytes secrete, and why does it matter?** Surfactant, which lowers surface tension and prevents alveolar collapse.
4. **Name the components of the blood–air barrier.** Surfactant film, type I pneumocyte, fused basal laminae, capillary endothelium.
5. **How does cartilage change down the tree?** C-shaped rings (trachea) → irregular plates (bronchi) → absent (bronchioles).
6. **Comparison model:** Bronchi retain cartilage plates and glands; bronchioles lack both, typically showing a muscular wall.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch airway transition to respiratory bronchiole, alveolar duct and alveolus. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Type II pneumocytes synthesize surfactant; type I pneumocytes provide a large diffusion surface.

## Chapter 18 — Skin

1. **Name the epidermal layers from deep to superficial.** Basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum (thick skin only), corneum.
2. **Distinguish the developmental origins of three nonkeratinocyte epidermal cells.** Melanocytes arise from the neural crest; Merkel cells arise from epidermal progenitors (supported by mammalian lineage tracing); Langerhans cells are immune-lineage cells seeded mainly by embryonic yolk-sac and fetal-liver precursors.
3. **What is the function of lamellar bodies?** They discharge lipid into the intercellular space, forming the water barrier.
4. **Where are Meissner and Pacinian corpuscles?** Meissner in the dermal papillae (light touch); Pacinian deep in the dermis/hypodermis (pressure, vibration).
5. **How do sebaceous and eccrine glands differ in secretion?** Sebaceous: holocrine. Eccrine: merocrine.
6. **Comparison model:** Thick palmar/plantar skin has prominent corneum and no hair follicles; thin skin may contain hair follicles.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw epidermal strata and appendages; add stratum lucidum only where appropriate. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Melanocytes are neural-crest derived, Merkel cells are epidermal-lineage, and Langerhans cells are immune-lineage.

## Chapter 19 — The Urinary System

1. **Which nephron segment is most eosinophilic, and why?** The proximal convoluted tubule: abundant mitochondria and a brush border for active reabsorption.
2. **What are the three layers of the filtration barrier?** Fenestrated endothelium, glomerular basement membrane, and podocyte slit diaphragms.
3. **What does the macula densa sense, and what does the JGA release?** NaCl concentration; juxtaglomerular cells release renin.
4. **Which cells of the collecting duct respond to ADH?** Principal cells, by inserting aquaporins into the apical membrane.
5. **What makes urothelium a barrier?** Umbrella cells with uroplakin plaques and tight junctions.
6. **Comparison model:** PCT has a more prominent brush border, acidophilic cytoplasm and less open lumen; DCT shows a clearer lumen with scant brush border.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch a renal corpuscle with nearby PCT and DCT profiles. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** The glomerular filtration barrier cannot be resolved fully by routine light microscopy.

## Chapter 20 — Endocrine Glands

1. **Which pituitary cells are acidophils?** Somatotrophs (GH) and lactotrophs (prolactin).
2. **Where are oxytocin and ADH made and stored?** Made in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei; stored and released in the pars nervosa.
3. **Name the adrenal zones and their main hormones.** Glomerulosa: aldosterone; fasciculata: cortisol; reticularis: androgens.
4. **Which islet cells secrete insulin, and how abundant are they?** β cells; about twice as abundant as α cells.
5. **What are the two cell types of the parathyroid, and which secretes PTH?** Principal (chief) cells secrete PTH; oxyphil cells are larger, acidophilic, and increase with age.
6. **Comparison model:** Thyroid contains colloid-filled follicles; parathyroid has chief-cell cords and capillaries, not storage follicles.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch thyroid follicle, adrenal cortical zonation and parathyroid cell cords. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Thyroid C cells secrete calcitonin; thyroid follicular cells secrete T3/T4.

## Chapter 21 — The Male Reproductive System

1. **What forms the blood–testis barrier, and what does it separate?** Tight junctions between Sertoli cells; basal and adluminal compartments.
2. **Which hormone acts on Sertoli cells, and which on Leydig cells?** FSH on Sertoli; LH on Leydig.
3. **Name the four phases of spermiogenesis.** Golgi, cap, acrosome, maturation.
4. **What epithelium lines the epididymis, and what is its hallmark?** Pseudostratified columnar with stereocilia.
5. **What are corpora amylacea, and where are they found?** Calcified glycoprotein concretions in prostatic glands.
6. **Which prostatic zone gives rise to cancer, and which to benign hyperplasia?** Cancer: peripheral (70% of the gland). Benign hyperplasia: transition (5%).
7. **What cools the testicular arterial blood, and how?** The pampiniform plexus, by countercurrent heat exchange in the spermatic cord.
6. **Comparison model:** Sertoli cells support germ cells inside seminiferous tubules; Leydig cells produce testosterone in the interstitium.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw a seminiferous tubule from spermatogonia to spermatids and mark basement membrane. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Epididymal stereocilia are long actin-based microvilli, not motile cilia.

## Chapter 22 — The Female Reproductive System

1. **Which cells produce estrogen, and from what precursor?** Granulosa cells aromatize androgens from theca interna cells.
2. **What is released at ovulation, and at what meiotic stage?** The secondary oocyte, arrested in metaphase II, with the corona radiata.
3. **What prevents polyspermy?** Cortical-granule secretion modifies and hardens the zona pellucida; egg-membrane changes also reduce subsequent sperm fusion in mammals.
4. **Which hormone maintains the corpus luteum early in pregnancy?** hCG.
5. **How do proliferative and secretory endometrium differ?** Proliferative: straight narrow glands, mitoses. Secretory: tortuous dilated glands with secretion and edematous stroma.
6. **Comparison model:** Primordial follicles have a flattened follicular-cell layer; unilaminar primary follicles have cuboidal granulosa cells.
7. **Visual evidence:** Draw follicular development to corpus luteum, marking granulosa and theca. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Early follicular growth is largely gonadotropin independent; human polyspermy prevention is not proven to rely on a classic fast electrical block.

## Chapter 23 — The Eye & Ear: Special Sense Organs

1. **Name the five layers of the cornea, outside in.** Epithelium, Bowman membrane, stroma, Descemet membrane, endothelium.
2. **Where is aqueous humor produced and where does it drain?** Ciliary processes; drains through the trabecular meshwork into the canal of Schlemm.
3. **Which retinal cells form the optic nerve?** Ganglion cells.
4. **Why is acuity highest in the central foveola?** Its densely packed cones and laterally displaced inner retinal layers support precise, minimally obstructed sampling of the retinal image.
5. **Endolymph versus perilymph?** Endolymph (high K⁺) fills the membranous labyrinth; perilymph (high Na⁺) fills the bony labyrinth.
6. **Comparison model:** Rods are sensitive in dim light; cones support color and high-acuity daylight vision, highly concentrated at the foveola.
7. **Visual evidence:** Sketch retinal outer/inner layers and the cellular path of light vs neural output. Accept accurate labeling with anatomical relationships; do not demand photomicrograph-like color.
8. **Misconception model:** Avoid saying the entire fovea is wholly devoid of rods; the foveola is the central rod-free specialization.
