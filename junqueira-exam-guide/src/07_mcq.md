## Answer Key with Explanations

**1. B** — Basic dyes such as hematoxylin bind acidic structures, and nucleic acids (nucleus, rough ER) are the most acidic components of the cell. **Trap:** A — collagen is basic and binds eosin, not hematoxylin. C — hemoglobin is basic. D — elastin is not particularly acidic.

**2. C** — PAS stains the glycoprotein-rich basement membrane magenta; eosin stains it only weakly and orcein stains elastin. **Trap:** A — eosin stains protein, not carbohydrate. B — orcein stains elastin. D — Sudan black stains lipid.

**3. B** — A frozen section gives a diagnosis within minutes, at the cost of some morphological distortion. **Trap:** A takes a day or more. C and D are research or teaching preparations, not urgent diagnostics.

**4. C** — The light microscope resolves about 0.2 µm; electron microscopy is required for anything smaller. **Trap:** A is electron-microscope resolution. B and D are far too coarse.

**5. C** — Immunohistochemistry uses a specific antibody, visualized by an enzyme reaction or a fluorophore. **Trap:** A detects newly synthesized molecules. B detects nucleic acid sequence. D detects birefringence.

**6. B** — Steroid synthesis requires smooth ER and lipid (cholesterol) stores; such cells appear pale and foamy. **Trap:** A is the profile of a protein-secreting cell. C is a phagocyte. D is a stem or rapidly dividing cell.

**7. B** — Peroxisomes contain catalase and carry out β-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids. **Trap:** A is the lysosome (acid hydrolases). C degrades ubiquitinated proteins. D is the main ATP source.

**8. B** — Dynein moves cargo toward the minus end, i.e. toward the cell body (retrograde). **Trap:** A moves cargo the other way (anterograde). C is a muscle motor. D is wrong because transport is directional.

**9. C** — GFAP is the characteristic intermediate filament of astrocytes. **Trap:** A is muscle. B is connective tissue and some neural cells. D is the neuronal cytoskeleton.

**10. B** — The basal body is 9 + 0; the motile cilium and flagellum are 9 + 2. **Trap:** A and C are 9 + 2. D has no microtubules at all.

**11. A** — Lamin A mutations destabilize the nuclear envelope and cause Hutchinson–Gilford progeria. **Trap:** B is dystrophin. C is fibrillin. D is type IV collagen.

**12. B** — The acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, and 22 carry the nucleolar organizer regions. **Trap:** A and C are metacentric or submetacentric autosomes. D carries few rRNA genes.

**13. C** — The tight junction (zonula occludens) seals the intercellular space and creates polarity. **Trap:** A anchors cells to each other but does not seal. B communicates. D anchors epithelium to basal lamina.

**14. A** — Anti-desmoglein antibodies disrupt desmosomes and cause intraepithelial blistering. **Trap:** B links integrins to matrix (epidermolysis bullosa). C is a gap junction protein. D is a basal lamina protein.

**15. B** — The ureter is lined by transitional epithelium, which allows distension. **Trap:** A is the vagina and esophagus. C is the gallbladder. D is the trachea.

**16. B** — Long, stiff microvilli (stereocilia) are found in the epididymis and inner ear. **Trap:** A has motile cilia. C has motile cilia. D has microvilli of normal length.

**17. C** — The lamina reticularis contains type III (reticular) collagen, produced by fibroblasts. **Trap:** A is dense connective tissue. B is cartilage. D is the lamina densa.

**18. A** — Osteogenesis imperfecta results from defective type I collagen. **Trap:** B causes chondrodysplasias. C causes Ehlers–Danlos type IV. D causes Alport syndrome.

**19. B** — Vitamin C is required for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase; deficiency causes scurvy. **Trap:** A is needed for vision. C is calcium homeostasis. D is a cofactor for lysyl oxidase.

**20. B** — The plasma cell has an eccentric clock-face nucleus and a pale perinuclear Golgi zone. **Trap:** A has granules and a central nucleus. C has a bean-shaped nucleus and vacuoles. D has a bilobed nucleus.

**21. B** — UCP1 uncouples the proton gradient so that energy is released as heat. **Trap:** A is the normal function that UCP1 bypasses. C is unilocular white fat. D is a white-adipocyte hormone.

**22. B** — Adiponectin increases insulin sensitivity; it is reduced in obesity. **Trap:** A is leptin. C and D are not adiponectin actions.

**23. C** — Fibrocartilage has no perichondrium, which contributes to its poor healing. **Trap:** A and B have perichondrium. D is hyaline and does have perichondrium (though it is thin).

**24. C** — The annulus fibrosus is fibrocartilage, with type I and type II collagen. **Trap:** A is the articular surface. B is the epiglottis. D is tendon.

**25. C** — The osteoclast resorbs bone using a ruffled border and acid secretion. **Trap:** A forms bone. B maintains it. D is the precursor.

**26. B** — Osteoclasts arise from the monocyte–macrophage lineage, not from mesenchyme. **Trap:** A gives osteoblasts. C are the bone-forming cells themselves. D gives melanocytes and Schwann cells.

**27. C** — In the hypertrophic zone chondrocytes enlarge and the matrix begins to calcify. **Trap:** A is small, sparse cells. B is active division. D is where osteoid is deposited.

**28. C** — Calcitonin from thyroid C cells inhibits osteoclasts and lowers serum calcium. **Trap:** A raises calcium. B raises calcium by increasing absorption. D has no direct effect.

**29. B** — The primary center appears in the diaphysis; secondary centers appear in the epiphyses. **Trap:** A is a secondary center. C is the growth plate region. D is not an ossification center.

**30. C** — A multinucleated cell in a Howship lacuna is an osteoclast. **Trap:** A is cuboidal and lines the surface. B sits in a lacuna without a pit. D does not make Howship lacunae.

**31. B** — Dense regular connective tissue has parallel collagen bundles, as in tendon. **Trap:** A is dense irregular. C describes elastic tissue. D is reticular tissue.

**32. C** — Red bone marrow is the adult hematopoietic site. **Trap:** A is the fetal site. B is a lymphoid filter. D is a fat store, not hemopoietic.

**33. B** — Nissl bodies are stacked rough ER and polysomes. **Trap:** A describes steroid-producing cells. C is the axonal cytoskeleton. D are synaptic, not somatic.

**34. B** — One oligodendrocyte myelinates segments of several CNS axons. **Trap:** A is a supporting astrocyte. C myelinates in the PNS. D is phagocytic.

**35. C** — Microglia are mesodermal, derived from monocytes — the exception among glia. **Trap:** A gives melanocytes and Schwann cells. B gives neurons and macroglia. D gives epithelia.

**39. B** — Calcium binds troponin C, which moves tropomyosin and exposes the myosin-binding site. **Trap:** A is the smooth-muscle calcium sensor. C is the smooth-muscle target. D is a structural protein.

**41. B** — Cardiac muscle has one or two central nuclei; skeletal muscle nuclei are peripheral and multiple. **Trap:** A is shared. C and D are shared.

**43. B** — In mammalian skeletal muscle, triads lie at the A–I junction; in cardiac muscle, diads lie at the Z line. **Trap:** A is cardiac diads. C is the middle of the sarcomere. D is within the A band.

**44. A** — Type I fibers are slow, oxidative, and fatigue-resistant. **Trap:** B is fast, oxidative-glycolytic and fatigable. C is fast glycolytic. D is wrong.

**45. B** — Neutrophils constitute 60–70% of circulating leukocytes. **Trap:** A is second in adults. C and D are minorities.

**46. C** — The eosinophil has a bilobed nucleus and bright orange-red granules. **Trap:** A has basophilic granules. B has a multilobed nucleus and pale granules. D is agranular and larger.

**53. C** — Hassall corpuscles are concentric epithelial whorls unique to the thymic medulla. **Trap:** A has no corpuscles. B has follicles and sinuses. D has crypts and lymphoid tissue.

**54. B** — Neither the thymus nor the spleen has afferent lymphatics. **Trap:** A has afferent lymphatics. C and D are MALT, not encapsulated organs.

**55. B** — The PALS is the T-cell zone of the spleen; follicles are the B-cell zone. **Trap:** A occupies follicles. C and D are present but not the defining population.

**56. B** — Germinal centers are sites of B-cell proliferation and affinity maturation. **Trap:** A occurs in the thymus. C occurs in marrow. D occurs in marrow.

**57. B** — The red pulp filters blood and removes aged or damaged erythrocytes. **Trap:** A is thymic. C and D describe white pulp.

**58. C** — Erythrocytes circulate for about 120 days. **Trap:** A is a platelet. B and D are wrong.

**60. C** — Langerhans cells are the epidermal antigen-presenting cells; they originate in bone marrow. **Trap:** A is structural. B is pigment-producing. D is a mechanoreceptor.

# APPENDIX C — The Book's Stain Appendix (Light Microscopy Stains)

**Book pages 525–526 · `p4` · 138–139**

The textbook's own appendix (Appendix A in the source, pp 525–526) is short but examinable, because every plate caption in the atlas names its stain. This chapter reproduces its list with the chemistry behind each stain; the guide's own Appendix A (Chapter 24) is the quick-recall version.

| Stain | Chemistry | What it stains | Why it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hematoxylin & eosin (H&E)** | Hematoxylin is a **basic** dye; eosin is an **acidic** counterstain | Nuclei and rough ER blue-purple; collagen and most cytoplasmic proteins, especially mitochondria, pink-red | The default survey stain; the source of the basophilic/eosinophilic vocabulary |
| **Pararosaniline–toluidine blue (PT)** | Basic dye combination | Chromatin purple; cytoplasm and collagen lighter violet | Penetrates **plastic (resin) sections**, giving finer detail than H&E |
| **Mallory trichrome** | Sequential stain combination | Nuclei purple; cytoplasm, keratin, and erythrocytes red-orange; **collagen blue** | Demonstrates cells and small vessels of connective tissue. Masson and Gomori trichromes are equivalent, with collagen blue-green or green |
| **Picrosirius–hematoxylin (PSH)** | Sirius red in picric acid | Collagen red; cytoplasm pink-violet; nuclei purple | Collagen becomes **birefringent** under polarized light — a specific collagen stain |
| **Periodic acid–Schiff (PAS)** | Periodic acid oxidizes vicinal diols; Schiff reagent detects them | Carbohydrate-rich structures **magenta** | Mucin granules, glycogen, glycocalyx, basement membranes |
| **Wright–Giemsa** | Two related Romanowsky combinations | Nuclei purple; erythrocytes pink-orange; leukocyte granules differentially | Blood and bone-marrow smears |
| **Silver and gold impregnation** | Metal salts reduce on filaments | Nerve filaments and **reticulin (type III collagen)** dark brown or black | Largely replaced by immunohistochemistry |
| **Elastin stains** — Weigert resorcin fuchsin, aldehyde fuchsin, orcein | Dye binds elastin | Elastic fibers brown or purple | Distinguishing elastic from collagen (aorta, lung, skin) |
| **Lipid stains** — oil red O, Sudan black, osmium tetroxide | Lipophilic dye partitions into lipid | Lipid droplets and myelin | Requires frozen sections or special processing, because solvents used in paraffin embedding extract lipid |
| **Other aniline dyes** — azures, cresyl violet, brilliant cresyl blue, luxol fast blue, light green | Basic dyes | Various cellular and extracellular components | Permanence and colour contrast in paraffin sections; many began as textile dyes |

> **MICRO** — Read the caption before you read the picture. "PT" tells you the section is plastic-embedded and shows fine detail; "Mallory" tells you collagen will be blue and cytoplasm red; "PSH" tells you collagen is red and can be examined under polarized light. The stain is part of the evidence.
