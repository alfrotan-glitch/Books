## Answer Key with Explanations

**1. A** — Basic dyes such as hematoxylin bind acidic structures, and nucleic acids (nucleus, rough ER) are the most acidic components of the cell. **Trap:** D — collagen is basic and binds eosin, not hematoxylin. B — hemoglobin is basic. C — elastin is not particularly acidic.

**2. C** — PAS stains the glycoprotein-rich basement membrane magenta; eosin stains it only weakly and orcein stains elastin. **Trap:** A — eosin stains protein, not carbohydrate. B — orcein stains elastin. D — Sudan black stains lipid.

**3. D** — A frozen section gives a diagnosis within minutes, at the cost of some morphological distortion. **Trap:** C takes a day or more. A and B are research or teaching preparations, not urgent diagnostics.

**4. B** — The light microscope resolves about 0.2 µm; electron microscopy is required for anything smaller. **Trap:** D is electron-microscope resolution. A and C are far too coarse.

**5. C** — Immunohistochemistry uses a specific antibody, visualized by an enzyme reaction or a fluorophore. **Trap:** A detects newly synthesized molecules. B detects nucleic acid sequence. D detects birefringence.

**6. B** — Steroid synthesis requires smooth ER and lipid (cholesterol) stores; such cells appear pale and foamy. **Trap:** A is the profile of a protein-secreting cell. C is a phagocyte. D is a stem or rapidly dividing cell.

**7. C** — Peroxisomes contain catalase and carry out β-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids. **Trap:** B is the lysosome (acid hydrolases). D degrades ubiquitinated proteins. A is the main ATP source.

**8. D** — Dynein moves cargo toward the minus end, i.e. toward the cell body (retrograde). **Trap:** C moves cargo the other way (anterograde). A is a muscle motor. B is wrong because transport is directional.

**9. B** — GFAP is the characteristic intermediate filament of astrocytes. **Trap:** D is muscle. A is connective tissue and some neural cells. C is the neuronal cytoskeleton.

**10. A** — The basal body is 9 + 0; the motile cilium and flagellum are 9 + 2. **Trap:** D and B are 9 + 2. C has no microtubules at all.

**11. B** — Lamin A mutations destabilize the nuclear envelope and cause Hutchinson–Gilford progeria. **Trap:** C is dystrophin. D is fibrillin. A is type IV collagen.

**12. D** — The acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, and 22 carry the nucleolar organizer regions. **Trap:** C and A are metacentric or submetacentric autosomes. B carries few rRNA genes.

**13. D** — The tight junction (zonula occludens) seals the intercellular space and creates polarity. **Trap:** B anchors cells to each other but does not seal. C communicates. A anchors epithelium to basal lamina.

**14. C** — Anti-desmoglein antibodies disrupt desmosomes and cause intraepithelial blistering. **Trap:** D links integrins to matrix (epidermolysis bullosa). A is a gap junction protein. B is a basal lamina protein.

**15. D** — The ureter is lined by transitional epithelium, which allows distension. **Trap:** C is the vagina and esophagus. A is the gallbladder. B is the trachea.

**16. A** — Long, stiff microvilli (stereocilia) are found in the epididymis and inner ear. **Trap:** D has motile cilia. B has motile cilia. C has microvilli of normal length.

**17. C** — The lamina reticularis contains type III (reticular) collagen, produced by fibroblasts. **Trap:** A is dense connective tissue. B is cartilage. D is the lamina densa.

**18. D** — Osteogenesis imperfecta results from defective type I collagen. **Trap:** A causes chondrodysplasias. B causes Ehlers–Danlos type IV. C causes Alport syndrome.

**19. A** — Vitamin C is required for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase; deficiency causes scurvy. **Trap:** D is needed for vision. B is calcium homeostasis. C is a cofactor for lysyl oxidase.

**20. D** — The plasma cell has an eccentric clock-face nucleus and a pale perinuclear Golgi zone. **Trap:** C has granules and a central nucleus. A has a bean-shaped nucleus and vacuoles. B has a bilobed nucleus.

**21. B** — UCP1 uncouples the proton gradient so that energy is released as heat. **Trap:** A is the normal function that UCP1 bypasses. C is unilocular white fat. D is a white-adipocyte hormone.

**22. A** — Adiponectin increases insulin sensitivity; it is reduced in obesity. **Trap:** D is leptin. B and C are not adiponectin actions.

**23. B** — Fibrocartilage has no perichondrium, which contributes to its poor healing. **Trap:** D and A have perichondrium. C is hyaline and is covered by perichondrium.

**24. D** — The annulus fibrosus is fibrocartilage, with type I and type II collagen. **Trap:** B is the articular surface. C is the epiglottis. A is tendon.

**25. B** — The osteoclast resorbs bone using a ruffled border and acid secretion. **Trap:** D forms bone. A maintains it. C is the precursor.

**26. A** — Osteoclasts arise from the monocyte–macrophage lineage, not from mesenchyme. **Trap:** D gives osteoblasts. B are the bone-forming cells themselves. C gives melanocytes and Schwann cells.

**27. D** — In the hypertrophic zone chondrocytes enlarge and the matrix begins to calcify. **Trap:** B is small, sparse cells. C is active division. A is where osteoid is deposited.

**28. B** — Calcitonin from thyroid C cells inhibits osteoclasts and lowers serum calcium. **Trap:** D raises calcium. A raises calcium by increasing absorption. C has no direct effect.

**29. A** — The primary center appears in the diaphysis; secondary centers appear in the epiphyses. **Trap:** D is a secondary center. B is the growth plate region. C is not an ossification center.

**30. B** — A multinucleated cell in a Howship lacuna is an osteoclast. **Trap:** D is cuboidal and lines the surface. A sits in a lacuna without a pit. C does not make Howship lacunae.

**31. A** — Dense regular connective tissue has parallel collagen bundles, as in tendon. **Trap:** D is dense irregular. B describes elastic tissue. C is reticular tissue.

**32. C** — Red bone marrow is the adult hematopoietic site. **Trap:** A is the fetal site. B is a lymphoid filter. D is a fat store, not hemopoietic.

**33. A** — Nissl bodies are stacked rough ER and polysomes. **Trap:** D describes steroid-producing cells. B is the axonal cytoskeleton. C are synaptic, not somatic.

**34. D** — One oligodendrocyte myelinates segments of several CNS axons. **Trap:** C is a supporting astrocyte. A myelinates in the PNS. B is phagocytic.

**35. B** — Microglia are mesodermal, derived from monocytes — the exception among glia. **Trap:** D gives melanocytes and Schwann cells. A gives neurons and macroglia. C gives epithelia.

**36. C** — Tight junctions between endothelial cells are the anatomical basis; astrocyte feet and pericytes reinforce it. **Trap:** B contributes a barrier but is not the seal. D lines ventricles. A support the capillary.

**37. B** — Endoneurium surrounds individual nerve fibers; perineurium surrounds fascicles. **Trap:** D surrounds the whole nerve. A surrounds a fascicle. C does not exist as a standard layer.

**38. C** — The A band is the length of the thick filaments and does not change during contraction. **Trap:** A and B shorten. D is wrong because the A band is fixed.

**39. D** — Calcium binds troponin C, which moves tropomyosin and exposes the myosin-binding site. **Trap:** C is the smooth-muscle calcium sensor. A is the smooth-muscle target. B is a structural protein.

**40. D** — Tight junctions are not a component of intercalated discs. **Trap:** A, B, and C are all present. Tight junctions are not a component of the intercalated disc.

**41. C** — Cardiac muscle has one or two central nuclei; skeletal muscle nuclei are peripheral and multiple. Striations, myofibrils, and mitochondria are shared, so they cannot distinguish the two. **Trap:** B is shared. D and A are shared.

**42. D** — Smooth muscle uses calmodulin and myosin light-chain kinase; it has no troponin. **Trap:** C refers to striated muscle. A is part of the thin filament but not the calcium sensor. B is wrong.

**43. C** — In mammalian skeletal muscle, triads lie at the A–I junction; in cardiac muscle, diads lie at the Z line. **Trap:** B is cardiac diads. D is the middle of the sarcomere. A is within the B band.

**44. B** — Type I fibers are slow, oxidative, and fatigue-resistant. **Trap:** C is fast, oxidative-glycolytic and fatigable. D is fast glycolytic. A is wrong.

**45. A** — Neutrophils constitute 60–70% of circulating leukocytes. **Trap:** D is second in adults. B and C are minorities.

**46. A** — The eosinophil has a bilobed nucleus and bright orange-red granules. **Trap:** C has basophilic granules. D has a multilobed nucleus and pale granules. B is agranular and larger.

**47. D** — The monocyte is the largest leukocyte and has a kidney-shaped nucleus. **Trap:** C has a round dark nucleus and scant cytoplasm. A has a segmented nucleus. B is smaller with coarse granules.

**48. B** — Platelets are fragments of megakaryocyte cytoplasm. **Trap:** A is an erythroid precursor. C and D do not make platelets.

**49. A** — Peritubular interstitial cells of the kidney produce erythropoietin. **Trap:** D produces it in fetal life. B and C are not the main source.

**50. C** — The normal M:E ratio is 2:1 to 4:1. This ratio is a standard hematology figure; the chapter's text does not state one, so treat it as supplementary. **Trap:** B, D, and A are not the normal adult ratio.

**51. A** — IgA is the main secretory immunoglobulin; it exists as a dimer with a secretory component. **Trap:** C crosses the placenta and dominates serum. D is the first antibody response. B mediates allergy.

**52. C** — CD4⁺ helper T cells recognize antigen on MHC class II. **Trap:** B is read by CD8 cells. D is not a classical antigen-presenting molecule. A is wrong.

**53. A** — Hassall corpuscles are concentric epithelial whorls unique to the thymic medulla. **Trap:** C has no corpuscles. D has follicles and sinuses. B has crypts and lymphoid tissue.

**54. B** — Neither the thymus nor the spleen has afferent lymphatics. **Trap:** A has afferent lymphatics. C and D are MALT, not encapsulated organs.

**55. C** — The PALS is the T-cell zone of the spleen; follicles are the B-cell zone. **Trap:** B occupies follicles. D and A are present but not the defining population.

**56. D** — Germinal centers are sites of B-cell proliferation and affinity maturation. **Trap:** C occurs in the thymus. A occurs in marrow. B occurs in marrow.

**57. C** — The red pulp filters blood and removes aged or damaged erythrocytes. **Trap:** B is thymic. D and A describe white pulp.

**58. A** — Erythrocytes circulate for about 120 days. **Trap:** C is a platelet. D and B are wrong.

**59. A** — In the second trimester, the liver and spleen are the main hemopoietic organs. **Trap:** D is the earliest site. B and C are later or lymphoid.

**60. C** — Langerhans cells are the epidermal antigen-presenting cells; they originate in bone marrow. **Trap:** A is structural. B is pigment-producing. D is a mechanoreceptor.
