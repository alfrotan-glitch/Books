## Answer Key with Explanations

**1. B** — Basic dyes such as hematoxylin bind acidic structures, and nucleic acids (nucleus, rough ER) are the most acidic components of the cell.

**2. C** — PAS stains the glycoprotein-rich basement membrane magenta; eosin stains it only weakly and orcein stains elastin.

**3. B** — A frozen section gives a diagnosis within minutes, at the cost of some morphological distortion.

**4. C** — The light microscope resolves about 0.2 µm; electron microscopy is required for anything smaller.

**5. C** — Immunohistochemistry uses a specific antibody, visualized by an enzyme reaction or a fluorophore.

**6. B** — Steroid synthesis requires smooth ER and lipid (cholesterol) stores; such cells appear pale and foamy.

**7. B** — Peroxisomes contain catalase and carry out β-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids.

**8. B** — Dynein moves cargo toward the minus end, i.e. toward the cell body (retrograde).

**9. C** — GFAP is the characteristic intermediate filament of astrocytes.

**10. B** — The basal body is 9 + 0; the motile cilium and flagellum are 9 + 2.

**11. A** — Lamin A mutations destabilize the nuclear envelope and cause Hutchinson–Gilford progeria.

**12. B** — The acrocentric chromosomes 13, 14, 15, 21, and 22 carry the nucleolar organizer regions.

**13. C** — The tight junction (zonula occludens) seals the intercellular space and creates polarity.

**14. A** — Anti-desmoglein antibodies disrupt desmosomes and cause intraepithelial blistering.

**15. B** — The ureter is lined by transitional epithelium, which allows distension.

**16. B** — Long, stiff microvilli (stereocilia) are found in the epididymis and inner ear.

**17. C** — The lamina reticularis contains type III (reticular) collagen, produced by fibroblasts.

**18. A** — Osteogenesis imperfecta results from defective type I collagen.

**19. B** — Vitamin C is required for prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase; deficiency causes scurvy.

**20. B** — The plasma cell has an eccentric clock-face nucleus and a pale perinuclear Golgi zone.

**21. B** — UCP1 uncouples the proton gradient so that energy is released as heat.

**22. B** — Adiponectin increases insulin sensitivity; it is reduced in obesity.

**23. C** — Fibrocartilage has no perichondrium, which contributes to its poor healing.

**24. C** — The annulus fibrosus is fibrocartilage, with type I and type II collagen.

**25. C** — The osteoclast resorbs bone using a ruffled border and acid secretion.

**26. B** — Osteoclasts arise from the monocyte–macrophage lineage, not from mesenchyme.

**27. C** — In the hypertrophic zone chondrocytes enlarge and the matrix begins to calcify.

**28. C** — Calcitonin from thyroid C cells inhibits osteoclasts and lowers serum calcium.

**29. B** — The primary center appears in the diaphysis; secondary centers appear in the epiphyses.

**30. C** — A multinucleated cell in a Howship lacuna is an osteoclast.

**31. B** — Dense regular connective tissue has parallel collagen bundles, as in tendon.

**32. C** — Red bone marrow is the adult hematopoietic site.

**33. B** — Nissl bodies are stacked rough ER and polysomes.

**34. B** — One oligodendrocyte myelinates segments of several CNS axons.

**35. C** — Microglia are mesodermal, derived from monocytes — the exception among glia.

**36. B** — Tight junctions between endothelial cells are the anatomical basis; astrocyte feet and pericytes reinforce it.

**37. C** — Endoneurium surrounds individual nerve fibers; perineurium surrounds fascicles.

**38. C** — The A band is the length of the thick filaments and does not change during contraction.

**39. B** — Calcium binds troponin C, which moves tropomyosin and exposes the myosin-binding site.

**40. D** — Tight junctions are not a component of intercalated discs.

**41. B** — Cardiac muscle has one or two central nuclei; skeletal muscle nuclei are peripheral and multiple.

**42. B** — Smooth muscle uses calmodulin and myosin light-chain kinase; it has no troponin.

**43. B** — In mammalian skeletal muscle, triads lie at the A–I junction; in cardiac muscle, diads lie at the Z line.

**44. A** — Type I fibers are slow, oxidative, and fatigue-resistant.

**45. B** — Neutrophils constitute 60–70% of circulating leukocytes.

**46. C** — The eosinophil has a bilobed nucleus and bright orange-red granules.

**47. B** — The monocyte is the largest leukocyte and has a kidney-shaped nucleus.

**48. B** — Platelets are fragments of megakaryocyte cytoplasm.

**49. B** — Peritubular interstitial cells of the kidney produce erythropoietin.

**50. B** — The normal M:E ratio is 2:1 to 4:1.

**51. C** — IgA is the main secretory immunoglobulin; it exists as a dimer with a secretory component.

**52. B** — CD4⁺ helper T cells recognize antigen on MHC class II.

**53. C** — Hassall corpuscles are concentric epithelial whorls unique to the thymic medulla.

**54. B** — Neither the thymus nor the spleen has afferent lymphatics.

**55. B** — The PALS is the T-cell zone of the spleen; follicles are the B-cell zone.

**56. B** — Germinal centers are sites of B-cell proliferation and affinity maturation.

**57. B** — The red pulp filters blood and removes aged or damaged erythrocytes.

**58. C** — Erythrocytes circulate for about 120 days.

**59. B** — In the second trimester, the liver and spleen are the main hemopoietic organs.

**60. C** — Langerhans cells are the epidermal antigen-presenting cells; they originate in bone marrow.

**61. C** — Auerbach's plexus lies between the circular and longitudinal layers of the muscularis externa.

**62. B** — Parietal cells secrete hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor.

**63. A** — Brunner glands lie in the submucosa of the duodenum.

**64. C** — Peyer's patches are characteristic of the ileum.

**65. B** — Paneth cells secrete lysozyme and defensins, contributing to innate immunity.

**66. C** — Villi are present in the small intestine and absent in the colon.

**67. D** — The central vein is at the center of the lobule, not in the portal triad.

**68. B** — Kupffer cells are the resident macrophages of the hepatic sinusoids.

**69. B** — Bile flows toward the portal triad while blood flows away from it toward the central vein.

**70. B** — The exocrine pancreas has basophilic acini with eosinophilic zymogen granules.

**71. B** — Beta cells secrete insulin and are about twice as abundant as alpha cells.

**72. B** — The esophagus is lined by stratified squamous non-keratinized epithelium.

**73. B** — Type II pneumocytes contain lamellar bodies and secrete surfactant.

**74. C** — Clara (club) cells line bronchioles.

**75. B** — Cartilage forms rings in the trachea, plates in bronchi, and is absent in bronchioles.

**76. D** — Type II pneumocytes do not form part of the thin blood–air barrier.

**77. D** — The stratum lucidum is present only in thick skin of palms and soles.

**78. B** — Melanocytes derive from the neural crest.

**79. C** — Sebaceous glands are holocrine; the whole cell disintegrates into the secretion.

**80. C** — Pacinian corpuscles lie deep in the dermis and hypodermis and sense vibration.

**81. C** — Keratinocytes make up about 90% of epidermal cells.

**82. B** — Meissner corpuscles in the dermal papillae mediate fine tactile discrimination.

**83. A** — Nephrin is the key protein of the slit diaphragm.

**84. C** — The macula densa is the thickened plaque of the distal straight tubule (thick ascending limb) where it touches the vascular pole.

**85. B** — Juxtaglomerular cells of the afferent arteriole secrete renin.

**86. A** — The proximal tubule is intensely eosinophilic because of abundant mitochondria and brush border.

**87. B** — The ureter is lined by transitional epithelium.

**88. B** — Thyroid follicles are lined by a simple epithelium whose cells range from squamous (inactive) to low columnar (active), classically described as cuboidal.

**89. B** — Parafollicular C cells secrete calcitonin.

**90. B** — The zona fasciculata secretes cortisol under ACTH control.

**91. B** — The adrenal medulla is neural crest derived; the cortex is mesodermal.

**92. B** — Herring bodies are neurosecretory dilatations in the posterior pituitary.

**93. B** — Tight junctions between adjacent Sertoli cells form the blood–testis barrier.

**94. B** — LH stimulates Leydig cells; FSH acts on Sertoli cells.

**95. B** — Fertilization normally occurs in the ampulla of the uterine tube.

**96. B** — Straight, narrow glands with mitoses are typical of the proliferative phase.

**97. B** — The corpus luteum secretes mainly progesterone.

**98. B** — The fovea contains cones only, giving maximal acuity and color vision.

**99. B** — Aqueous humor drains through the trabecular meshwork into the canal of Schlemm.

**100. A** — The inner and outer hair cells of the organ of Corti are the transducing receptors.
