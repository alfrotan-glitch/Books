# PART VI — RAPID REVIEW AND REFERENCE

## 24 · The Last-24-Hours Rapid Review

> **PEARL** — This chapter is designed to be read in one sitting, twice. Read it once slowly, then again out loud. Everything in it is either a rule that generates other facts or a fact that is asked almost every year.

### The book as a concept map

If you remember nothing else, remember the shape of the subject. Each level is generated by the level above it, and each arrow is a question an examiner can ask.

- **Molecules** — proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, nucleic acids
  - **Organelles** — membrane, cytosol, nucleus (Chapter 2–3)
    - **Cells** — each specialized by which organelle it expands
      - **Epithelium** — sits on a basal lamina, avascular, joined by junctions (Chapter 4)
        - **Surface epithelium** — number of layers and shape of the top cell
        - **Glandular epithelium** — merocrine, apocrine, holocrine
      - **Connective tissue** — defined by its matrix (Chapter 5)
        - **Loose / dense / reticular / elastic / adipose** — amount and arrangement of fibers
        - **Cartilage and bone** — matrix stiffened by proteoglycan or mineral (Chapters 7–8)
      - **Muscle** — converts ATP into movement (Chapter 10)
        - **Skeletal** — voluntary, peripheral nuclei, triads at the A–I junction
        - **Cardiac** — branched, central nucleus, intercalated discs, diads at the Z line
        - **Smooth** — no striations, calmodulin, dense bodies
      - **Nervous tissue** — converts stimulus into impulse (Chapter 9)
        - **Neuron** — soma, dendrites, axon, synapse
        - **Glia** — oligodendrocyte, Schwann, astrocyte, microglia, ependyma
  - **Organs** — one tissue arrangement plus a supporting stroma
    - **Tubes** — concentric layers (gut, vessels, ducts, ureter)
    - **Solid organs** — capsule, cortex, medulla, hilum (kidney, ovary, lymph node)
    - **Glands** — ducts or no ducts (exocrine versus endocrine)
  - **Systems** — organs that share a transport medium
    - **Circulatory** — heart, vessels, blood, lymph (Chapters 11–13)
    - **Immune** — thymus, lymph node, spleen, MALT (Chapter 14)
    - **Digestive** — gut tube plus liver, pancreas, salivary glands (Chapters 15–16)
    - **Respiratory** — conducting airway plus alveoli (Chapter 17)
    - **Integumentary** — skin and appendages (Chapter 18)
    - **Urinary** — filtration, reabsorption, storage (Chapter 19)
    - **Endocrine** — pituitary, thyroid, adrenal, islets, parathyroid, pineal (Chapter 20)
    - **Reproductive** — testis and ovary, then ducts and glands (Chapters 21–22)
    - **Special senses** — eye and ear (Chapter 23)

> **PEARL** — Read the map downward when you revise a chapter, and upward when you face an unknown slide. Downward gives you the function; upward gives you the name.

### The 60-second organ identification algorithm

**Step 1 — Is there a lumen?**
- **No lumen, solid cords or follicles** → thyroid (follicles), adrenal (cords), liver (cords + central veins), parathyroid (cords), pituitary (cell sheets), lymphoid organ (nodules).
- **Yes, a lumen** → go to Step 2.

**Step 2 — What is the epithelium?**
- **Stratified squamous** → esophagus, oral cavity, vagina, anal canal, epidermis (no lumen).
- **Simple columnar with crypts** → stomach, intestine, gallbladder, uterus.
- **Pseudostratified ciliated** → respiratory tract, epididymis, vas deferens.
- **Transitional** → ureter, bladder, renal pelvis.
- **Simple cuboidal/columnar tubules** → kidney, salivary ducts, epididymis.

**Step 3 — What surrounds it?**
- **Cartilage rings** → trachea. **Cartilage plates** → bronchus. **No cartilage** → bronchiole.
- **Three muscle layers** → stomach. **Taeniae coli** → colon. **Thick muscle wall** → vas deferens, ureter.
- **Serosa** → intraperitoneal; **adventitia** → retroperitoneal.

### Confessions of the examiner — the eight most common errors

1. Saying the osteoclast is mesenchymal. It is **hematopoietic**.
2. Saying the A band shortens. It **does not**.
3. Saying the thymus has follicles. It **does not**.
4. Saying the spleen has afferent lymphatics. It **does not**.
5. Saying the adrenal medulla makes steroids. It makes **catecholamines**.
6. Saying FSH acts on Leydig cells. It acts on **Sertoli** cells; LH acts on Leydig.
7. Saying rods are at the fovea. The fovea has **cones only**.
8. Saying the stomach has goblet cells. It has **surface mucous cells**, not goblet cells.
