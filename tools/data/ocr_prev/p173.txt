## Chapter 1

allow {?/3’lau/} v. to let, permit: We allow our son to drive the family car.

consider /kan’sida/ v. an opinion of what something or someone is: I consider Barbara my best friend.

employ {?/im’ploi/} v. to pay someone to work for you: The company employs 18 people. no longer /nou 'la:nga/ expression adv. not any more: Bobby is tired and no longer wants to go to the park.

prohibit /prou’hibat/ v. to forbid, to ban by order or law: The law prohibits people from killing each other.

retirement /ri’tayamont/ x. the period of your life when you stop working completely: My dad will reach retirement age soon.

significant /sig’nifikant/ adj. something that is important or shows something: On New Year’s Day people plan ways to make significant changes to their lives.

transition {?/tren’zifon/} n. a change from one condition to another: The transition from high school to college can be difficult for young people.

## Chapter 2

freedom {?/'fri:xdom/} n. having the power to do what you want: The dog is not in a cage and has the freedom to go wherever he wants to go.

gradually {?/'greedzowal:i/} adv. happening slowly or by small steps: Gradually, 1 got used to life in the city.

independent {?/,inda’pendant/} adj. free, able to do things by yourself and in your own way: Claire spent her last day in Japan independent of her boyfriend.

interview /'inta vyu:/ v. to ask a person questions to get information: A TV reporter interviewed the mayor about the city’s problems.

migrate /'mai greit/ v. to move to a different country or place: Birds migrate from cold to warm areas of the world each year.

overcome {?/} {?1 ouv r kAm/} v. to deal with and solve a problem: Not having much space in a store can be overcome by using tall shelves.

reluctant /ri’laktant/ adj. to be unwilling to do something: I’m a bit reluctant to start riding a scooter; I’d prefer to drive a car.

suburb /'sa bab/ n. a small city or town outside a large city: There are many cars in the suburbs.
